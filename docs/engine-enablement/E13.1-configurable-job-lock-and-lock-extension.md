# ZenBPM feature: configurable job lock per subscription, lock extension, per-subscription active-job cap

Story for the `zenbpm` engine (`github.com/pbinitiative/zenbpm`), analysed against commit `33a0906f`
(2026-09-19). It closes GAPS 2 and 3 of the adapter plan and answers open question 2 ("high
priority"). Paths are relative to the `zenbpm/` repository unless prefixed.

> **Status: implemented.** Merged to `zenbpm` `main` as commit `071460cc` ("#841: Implement job
> locking capability per subscription", pull request #842) on 2026-09-23, first release carrying it:
> the next tag after `v1.7.0` (`VERSION` says `v1.8.0`). Sections 1-9 below are the story as it was
> written and stay as the record of the design; section 0 says what shipped and where it differs.

## 0. What shipped

Everything of sections 4 and 8, with the section 9 decisions taken as follows:

| Decision (section 9) | Taken |
|---|---|
| 1. Per-type versus per-client cap | per (client, job type) only; no additional per-client total. The old per-client total is documented as an "Upgrade note" in `docs/reference/jobs.md` (the repository has no release-notes file) |
| 2. Extension relative to now or additive | relative to now |
| 3. REST extension endpoint | kept: `POST /v1/jobs/{jobKey}/extend-lock` |
| 4. Ownership | left out; a DEBUG line in `jobmanager` `releaseLock` records when a job is completed or failed by a client other than the lock holder |

Details the implementation added beyond the story, which the adapter relies on:

- **Error codes.** `ErrorResult.code` of an extension is `JOB_STREAM_ERROR_CODE_LOCK_NOT_HELD` (1),
  `JOB_STREAM_ERROR_CODE_LOCK_HELD_BY_OTHER_CLIENT` (2), or `JOB_STREAM_ERROR_CODE_LEADER_UNAVAILABLE`
  (3) when the partition leader could not be reached or just changed. Code 3 is NOT a refusal: the
  extension may have been applied, so the only deadline to trust is the last confirmed one. REST maps
  1 and 2 to `409`, an unknown key to `404`, code 3 to `502` ("retry in a moment").
- **A duration of any positive length is accepted** and counted from now, capped silently at
  `jobManager.maxLockDurationMs`. A capped request is visible only in the answered deadline.
- **Closing the stream releases every lock of that client at once** (`removeClient` drops its
  distributed jobs), so a worker which disconnects does not make its jobs wait for their deadline.
- **A leader holds at most about 32,700 locked jobs** across all clients and types: every locked key
  is a parameter of the query loading the next batch, and SQLite accepts 32,766. At that bound the
  leader logs a warning and delivers again as locks lapse (`internal/sql/limits.go`).
- **`lock_until` is conservative**: the leader counts the lock from the end of the send and reports a
  deadline taken just before it, so the real deadline is never earlier than the reported one. The
  docs recommend renewing at the latest at half the remaining time, or counting from receipt with the
  subscribed duration where the clocks cannot be trusted.
- **Go client**: `RegisterWorkerWithOptions`, `WithJobType`, `WithLockDuration`, `WithMaxActiveJobs`,
  `Worker.ExtendLock`, `zenclient.ErrLeaderUnavailable`; no automatic renewal.
- Tests: unit tests in `internal/cluster/jobmanager/*lock*_test.go`, `internal/grpc/server_lock_test.go`,
  `internal/cluster/server/extend_job_lock_test.go`, `internal/rest/lock_duration_test.go`,
  `pkg/zenclient/grpc_lock_test.go`; e2e `test/e2e/grpc_job_lock_test.go` and
  `test/e2e/job_extend_lock_rest_test.go`.

The adapter twin of section 10 is not a separate story any more: the adapter is built against an
engine carrying this feature from its first story, and the plan's epics 2, 6 and 8 were rewritten on
2026-09-23 to use it directly (see `docs/plan/epic-13-engine-enablement.md`, E13.1).

## 1. Why

A worker which receives a job over the gRPC `JobStream` holds it for exactly 30 seconds. After that
the partition leader hands the same job to the next subscribed client, whether or not the first one
is still working on it. Nothing a worker can send keeps the lock alive, and every client may hold at
most ten jobs at once across all job types it subscribed to. Three consequences for any worker, and
for the VanillaBP adapter in particular:

1. A handler which legitimately runs longer than 30 seconds (a slow downstream API, a large database
   transaction) meets a second delivery of its own job on another worker while it is still running.
   The application then runs the same business logic twice at the same time.
2. A task which stays open by design - VanillaBP's asynchronous `@TaskId` task, which the application
   completes hours or days later through the REST API - is redelivered every 30 seconds for as long as
   it is open. Each redelivery costs a round trip and occupies one of the ten slots, so ten open
   asynchronous tasks starve every other job type of that client.
3. The ten-slot cap is per client, not per job type, so one slow job type takes the capacity of all
   the others.

The adapter plan works around all three (`docs/architecture/02-design-decisions.md`, decision 9, and
`docs/plan/epic-06-task-processing.md`), at the price of documented double deliveries and a hard
"keep handlers under 30 seconds" rule for users. This feature removes the need for the workaround.

## 2. Current behaviour, with the code it lives in

| What | Where | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| Lock duration | `internal/cluster/jobmanager/server.go:27` | `jobLockDuration = 30 * time.Second`, a package constant |
| Active-job cap | `internal/cluster/jobmanager/server.go:29`, `:130` | `maxActiveJobsPerClient = 10`; `clients[client] = maxActiveJobsPerClient` once per distribution round, decremented per outstanding job of ANY type |
| The lock itself | `internal/cluster/jobmanager/server.go:42-46`, `:134-151` | `distributedJob{sentTime, client, jobKey}` in an in-memory slice on the partition leader; an entry older than the constant is dropped at the start of every round, which makes the job loadable again |
| What the leader loads | `internal/sql/queries/job.sql` `GetWaitingJobs` | `state = 1 AND type IN (...) AND key NOT IN (distributed)`; the database knows nothing about locks |
| Release on completion / failure | `server.go` `completeJob`, `failJob` | the entry is removed after the engine call succeeded; the `clientID` argument is accepted and ignored - there is no ownership check |
| REST completion | `internal/cluster/node.go:916` `CompleteJob` | goes to the leader's `ZenService.CompleteJob` with no `client_id`; the lock entry stays until it expires (harmless: a completed job is no longer `state = 1`) |
| Wire contract | `pkg/zenclient/proto/zenbpm.proto` | `StreamSubscriptionRequest` carries `lock_duration` and `max_active_jobs` as COMMENTED-OUT fields 3 and 4; `WaitingJob` carries no deadline |
| Internal relay | `internal/cluster/proto/zen_cluster.proto` `SubscribeJobRequest` | `job_type`, `type`, `client_id` only; the client node (`jobmanager/client.go`) replays subscriptions to every partition leader |
| Go client | `pkg/zenclient/grpc.go` | `RegisterWorker(ctx, clientID, f, jobTypes...)`, no options, no way to extend a lock |
| Docs | `docs/reference/jobs.md` | "These jobs are locked for 30s and will not be distributed to another client in that time." |
| Known TODO | `jobmanager/client.go:55` | `// TODO: add mechanism to handle max_active_jobs and lock_duration` |

Two properties of the current design are kept on purpose: the lock is in memory on the partition
leader (a leader change forgets every lock, and the new leader redelivers at once), and completion
is not bound to the lock holder (a job may be completed by anybody who knows its key, including a
REST client which never held the lock). Both are named in section 8.

## 3. Goal and non-goals

**Goal.** A worker decides, per subscription, how long a delivered job stays locked and how many jobs
of that type it may hold at once; it can extend the lock of a job it holds before the lock lapses;
it learns the lock deadline with every delivery; and the engine's defaults for both values are
configurable. Existing clients keep working unchanged.

**Non-goals** (each is a possible follow-up, none blocks the adapter):

- persisting locks so they survive a partition leader change;
- refusing a completion or failure from a client which does not hold the lock ("ownership");
- a lock for jobs fetched through REST `GET /v1/jobs`, which stays a plain query;
- changing the round-robin distribution itself.

## 4. Design

### 4.1 Vocabulary

- **Lock duration**: how long a delivered job is reserved for the client it was sent to. After it
  lapses the job is loadable again and may go to any subscribed client.
- **Lock deadline** (`lock_until`): the absolute instant, unix milliseconds on the leader's clock,
  at which the current lock lapses. Sent with every delivery and with every extension.
- **Active jobs**: jobs delivered to a client whose lock has not lapsed and which were neither
  completed nor failed. Counted per (client, job type).

### 4.2 Wire contract, public gRPC (`pkg/zenclient/proto/zenbpm.proto`)

```proto
message StreamSubscriptionRequest {
  enum Type { TYPE_UNDEFINED = 0; TYPE_SUBSCRIBE = 1; TYPE_UNSUBSCRIBE = 2; }
  string job_type = 1;
  Type type = 2;
  // How long a job of this type stays locked for this client after delivery, in milliseconds.
  // 0 = the engine's default (jobManager.defaultLockDurationMs). Capped at
  // jobManager.maxLockDurationMs; a larger value is capped, never rejected, and the effective
  // value is reported in lock_until of every delivery.
  int64 lock_duration_ms = 3;
  // How many jobs of this type this client may hold at once. 0 = the engine's default
  // (jobManager.defaultMaxActiveJobs). Capped at jobManager.maxActiveJobsCap.
  int32 max_active_jobs = 4;
}

message JobExtendLockRequest {
  int64 key = 1;
  // 0 = extend by the lock_duration_ms of the subscription the job was delivered under.
  int64 lock_duration_ms = 2;
}

message JobStreamRequest {
  oneof request {
    StreamSubscriptionRequest subscription = 1;
    JobCompleteRequest complete = 2;
    JobFailRequest fail = 3;
    JobExtendLockRequest extend_lock = 4;
  }
}

message WaitingJob {
  // ... existing fields 1-7 unchanged ...
  int64 lock_until = 8;  // unix millis; lapse of the lock this delivery holds
}

message LockExtended {
  int64 key = 1;
  int64 lock_until = 2;
}

message JobStreamResponse {
  ErrorResult error = 1;
  WaitingJob job = 2;
  LockExtended lock_extended = 3;  // the answer to a JobExtendLockRequest
}
```

Field numbers 3 and 4 of `StreamSubscriptionRequest` are the ones already reserved by the comments.
`ErrorResult.code` is populated for the extension errors (section 4.6) so a client can act on it
without parsing text; today it is always `nil`.

A resubscription of the same (client, type) with other values REPLACES the values (the server already
tolerates a resubscribe, `server.go` `subscribeClient` "resubscribing the same client ... must not
register it twice"); jobs already delivered keep the deadline they were delivered with.

### 4.3 Wire contract, internal cluster gRPC (`internal/cluster/proto/zen_cluster.proto`)

- `SubscribeJobRequest` gains `int64 lock_duration_ms = 4; int32 max_active_jobs = 5;`, relayed
  verbatim by the client node (`jobmanager/client.go` `addJobSub`, `resendClientSubscriptions`).
- `InternalJob` gains `int64 lock_until = 9;`, set by the leader when it sends the job, copied into
  `jobmanager.Job` and into the public `WaitingJob`.
- New RPC `rpc ExtendJobLock(ExtendJobLockRequest) returns (ExtendJobLockResponse);` with
  `ExtendJobLockRequest{int64 key; string client_id; int64 lock_duration_ms}` and
  `ExtendJobLockResponse{ErrorResult error; int64 lock_until}`. Routed like `CompleteJob`: the
  public server asks the job manager, the client side resolves the partition leader from the key
  (`zenflake.GetPartitionId`), the leader's `ZenService` calls `JobManager.ExtendJobLock`.

### 4.4 The leader's job server (`internal/cluster/jobmanager/server.go`)

```go
type subscriptionSettings struct {
    lockDuration  time.Duration
    maxActiveJobs int
}

type distributedJob struct {
    client    ClientID
    jobKey    int64
    jobType   JobType
    lockUntil time.Time   // replaces sentTime + constant
}
```

- `subscriptions map[JobType]map[ClientID]*nodeSub` gains a sibling
  `settings map[JobType]map[ClientID]subscriptionSettings`, written by `subscribeClient` (defaults
  applied and caps enforced there, once) and deleted with the subscription.
- The distribution round (`distributeJobs`) changes in three places:
  1. release: an entry is dropped when `lockUntil.Before(now)` (was `sentTime + 30s`);
  2. capacity: `clients` becomes `map[clientAndType]int` seeded from `settings`, decremented per
     outstanding entry of THAT type; `jobsToLoad` is the sum over all (client, type) pairs; the
     round-robin candidate check reads the (candidate, type) capacity;
  3. send: the new entry is stored with `lockUntil = now + settings.lockDuration` and that value is
     put into `InternalJob.lock_until` before `stream.Send`.
- New method `extendLock(clientID ClientID, jobKey int64, duration time.Duration) (time.Time, error)`:
  under `distributedJobsMu`, find the entry with `jobKey`; none -> `ErrLockNotHeld` (the lock lapsed
  or the job was completed); held by another client -> `ErrLockHeldByOtherClient`; else `duration`
  0 -> the subscription's lock duration, cap applied, `lockUntil = now + duration`, return it. The
  extension is relative to NOW, not to the old deadline, so a worker renewing every `duration/2`
  keeps a stable lead.
- `completeJob` / `failJob` unchanged (they still remove the entry after the engine call).

The two exported errors live in `jobmanager` so the public server can map them to codes.

### 4.5 Client side and public server

- `jobmanager/client.go`: `addJobSub(ctx, clientID, jobType, settings)` stores the settings in
  `clientSub.jobTypes map[JobType]subscriptionSettings` (today `map[JobType]struct{}`) so the replay
  to a newly opened leader stream (`resendClientSubscriptions`) sends them too; `extendLock(ctx,
  clientID, key, duration)` resolves the leader by partition and calls the new RPC.
- `jobmanager/manager.go`: `AddClientJobSub` takes the settings; new `ExtendJobLockReq` (client
  side) and `ExtendJobLock` (leader side, `NodeIsNotALeader` when no server runs).
- `internal/grpc/server.go` `recvClientRequests`: `JobStreamRequest_ExtendLock` calls
  `ExtendJobLockReq` and answers `JobStreamResponse{LockExtended{key, lock_until}}` or
  `ErrorResult{code, message}`; the `Subscription` case passes `lock_duration_ms` and
  `max_active_jobs` on. `sendClientJobs` copies `lock_until` into `WaitingJob`.
- `internal/cluster/server/server.go`: `ExtendJobLock` RPC handler, shaped like `CompleteJob`.

### 4.6 REST (`openapi/api.yaml`, `internal/rest/server.go`, `internal/cluster/node.go`)

`POST /v1/jobs/{jobKey}/extend-lock`, body `{ "clientId": "...", "lockDuration": "PT5M" }`
(`clientId` required, the stream's client id; `lockDuration` optional ISO-8601 duration, absent =
the subscription's value), `200 { "lockUntil": "<date-time>" }`. Errors: `404` job unknown (checked
through `GetJob` first, so a wrong key is told apart from a lapsed lock), `409` lock not held or
held by another client (`zenerr.Conflict`, already exists in `internal/cluster/zenerr`), `502`
cluster. It exists for the worker shape the adapter uses - jobs delivered over the stream, every
command sent over REST - and for anybody whose stream handling lives elsewhere than the completion
code. Documented as such; REST-polled jobs hold no lock and cannot extend one.

### 4.7 Configuration (`internal/config`, `conf/zenbpm/*.yaml`, `docs/reference/configuration.md`)

New section `jobManager`:

| Field | Env | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `defaultLockDurationMs` | `JOB_MANAGER_DEFAULT_LOCK_DURATION_MS` | `30000` | applied where a subscription sends 0; today's constant |
| `maxLockDurationMs` | `JOB_MANAGER_MAX_LOCK_DURATION_MS` | `86400000` (24 h) | a subscription or extension asking for more is capped to this |
| `defaultMaxActiveJobs` | `JOB_MANAGER_DEFAULT_MAX_ACTIVE_JOBS` | `10` | applied where a subscription sends 0; today's constant |
| `maxActiveJobsCap` | `JOB_MANAGER_MAX_ACTIVE_JOBS_CAP` | `1000` | a subscription asking for more is capped |

Validation at startup: defaults within their caps, all values positive; a violation ends the start
naming the field and the environment variable (the style of the existing config validation).

### 4.8 Go client (`pkg/zenclient/grpc.go`)

Functional options, as `DEVELOPMENT.md` prescribes for optional arguments:

```go
func (c *Grpc) RegisterWorker(ctx context.Context, clientID string, f WorkerFunc, jobTypes ...string) (*Worker, error)
func (c *Grpc) RegisterWorkerWithOptions(ctx context.Context, clientID string, f WorkerFunc, opts ...WorkerOption) (*Worker, error)

func WithJobType(jobType string, subOpts ...SubscriptionOption) WorkerOption
func WithLockDuration(d time.Duration) SubscriptionOption
func WithMaxActiveJobs(n int) SubscriptionOption

func (w *Worker) ExtendLock(ctx context.Context, jobKey int64, d time.Duration) (lockUntil time.Time, err error)
```

`RegisterWorker` keeps its signature and behaviour (defaults). `WaitingJob.LockUntil` is available
to the `WorkerFunc` through the proto message. A `WorkerFunc` which needs longer than its lock calls
`ExtendLock`; the client does not renew automatically (a follow-up could add
`WithAutoExtend(before time.Duration)`). The Java client (`pbinitiative/zenbpm-java-client`) is a
separate repository and gets its own story once this lands.

## 5. Behaviour to specify precisely

1. A job delivered under a subscription with `lock_duration_ms = L` is not loaded by
   `GetWaitingJobs` again before `L` has passed (it stays in the skip list), and IS loaded again
   after `L` if neither completed, failed nor extended.
2. An extension at time `t` with duration `D` sets the deadline to `t + D`, whichever the previous
   deadline was; the response carries the new deadline; a subsequent round does not release the
   entry before it.
3. An extension for a key which is not in the distributed list answers `LOCK_NOT_HELD`; for a key
   held by another client `LOCK_HELD_BY_OTHER_CLIENT`; the job itself is untouched in both cases.
4. A client with `max_active_jobs = N` for type `T` never holds more than `N` outstanding jobs of
   `T`; outstanding jobs of another type do not count against `N`; the total the leader loads per
   round is the sum of the free capacities.
5. A subscription without the new fields behaves exactly as today: 30 s, 10 per client - with ONE
   deliberate difference: the ten are counted per job type now, not per client. This is a
   behavioural change for a client subscribed to several types (it may receive more jobs in total
   than before). It is called out in the release notes; a client which wants the old total sets
   `max_active_jobs` per type accordingly.
6. `lock_until` is present on every delivered job and is the leader's clock; a client compares it
   to the leader's time only through the difference to its own `time.Now()` at receipt (document the
   skew caveat).
7. A leader change forgets the locks and the new leader redelivers at once (unchanged; documented).
8. Completion or failure by a client which does not hold the lock still succeeds (unchanged);
   `completeJob` logs at DEBUG that the completing client differs from the lock holder, so the
   follow-up "ownership" story has data to look at.

## 9. Decisions to take before or while implementing

1. **Per-type versus per-client cap.** The design counts per (client, type), which is what the
   commented proto field says and what a mixed worker needs. If the maintainers prefer to keep a
   per-client total as well, add a fifth setting `jobManager.maxActiveJobsPerClient` (0 = unlimited)
   and enforce both; the default of that setting decides whether existing clients see a change.
2. **Extension relative to now, or additive.** Relative to now is chosen (a renewing worker gets a
   predictable lead; additive would let a burst of renewals push a deadline days out). If additive
   is preferred, cap the resulting lead by `maxLockDurationMs`.
3. **REST extension endpoint: keep or drop.** It exists for stream-delivered jobs completed over REST.
   Dropping it forces such workers to keep the stream in reach of their completion code.
4. **Ownership** (refusing complete/fail from a non-holder) stays a separate story; the DEBUG line of
   5.8 is its preparation.

## 10. What the adapter does once this ships (the adapter twin of E13.1)

- `vanillabp.adapters.<id>.job-timeout` (adapter, module, workflow, task; default `PT5M`) is sent
  as `lock_duration_ms` per subscribed type; conflicting task-level values for one job type fail the
  deployment guiding, as Camunda 8 does (`docs/plan/epic-06-task-processing.md`).
- `max-jobs-active` per subscription = the adapter's free handler slots, so the dispatcher's
  "drop when no slot" path (decision 9) becomes the exception rather than the rule.
- A `@WorkflowTask` handler renews its lock from a timing thread while it runs (Camunda 8's
  `Camunda8CommandRetry` reads the remaining lock from the job; here it reads `lock_until`).
- An asynchronous `@TaskId` task is delivered ONCE and its lock renewed by `async-task-lock-renewal`
  (default `PT1H`) instead of being redelivered every 30 s; the delivery record answers nothing any
  more because nothing is redelivered.
- Decision 9's second sentence and GAPS 2 and 3 are superseded; Deviations 2 is removed from the
  wiki; `docs/analysis/02-spi-requirements-mapping.md` rows "Task delivery", "Handler longer than
  the lock" and "Asynchronous task staying open" become SUPPORTED.
